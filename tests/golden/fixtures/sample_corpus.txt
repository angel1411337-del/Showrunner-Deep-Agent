The morning had dawned clear and cold over Winterfell, the great castle of the Northern realm. Jon stood at the battlements, his dark hair whipped by the wind, watching the kingsroad stretch south toward distant lands he had never seen. Beside him, his direwolf Ghost sat silent as snow, red eyes gleaming.

"You brood too much, brother," said Daenerys, ascending the stone steps to join him. The silver-haired woman wore the heavy furs of the North now, though she had been born in sunnier climes. In her hands she carried a small wooden box, worn smooth by age.

Jon turned to face her. "The raven came at dawn. Tyrion writes from King's Landing that the council grows restless. They speak of the old prophecy again."

"The prophecy of the Three Crowns," Daenerys murmured, her violet eyes distant. "When fire and ice unite beneath three crowns of gold, the long darkness shall lift, and the realm shall know ten thousand years of peace." She had heard those words in her fever dreams, spoken by voices she could not name.

The box in her hands seemed to grow heavier. Within it lay the Dragonstone Dagger, a blade of Valyrian steel with a ruby pommel that glowed faintly in shadow. Her ancestor Aegon had carried it during the Conquest. Now it waited, patient as stone, for a purpose none yet understood.

"There is something else," Jon said, lowering his voice. "Who sent the assassin to the Twins last moon? Someone tried to kill Lord Manderly, and the trail grows cold." The mystery gnawed at him. Three witnesses had died before they could speak, and the killer had vanished like morning mist.

Tyrion had written of this too, in his cramped hand: Follow the gold, not the blade. The Imp rarely spoke plainly, but his riddles often proved prophetic in their own way.

In the courtyard below, soldiers drilled with spear and shield. The castle smiths hammered at their forges, and servants hurried between the kitchens and the great hall. Winterfell had stood for eight thousand years, and its stones remembered every lord who had walked its corridors. Some said the walls themselves could whisper secrets to those who knew how to listen.

"We should ride to Moat Cailin tomorrow," Daenerys said at last. "If the answers lie anywhere, they lie in the South." She placed the wooden box in Jon's hands. "Keep the dagger safe. I fear it will be needed before this tale is done."

Ghost rose to his feet, ears pricked toward the wolfswood. Something moved in the shadows beneath the ancient trees, but when Jon looked, he saw only snow drifting between the branches. The direwolf growled low in his throat, a sound that made the hairs rise on the back of Jon's neck.

"Winter is coming," Jon said, the old words of his house. But this time, they felt less like a warning and more like a promise of something vast and unknowable approaching from the darkness beyond the Wall.

Above them, a single raven circled the tower, black wings stark against the pale morning sky.
